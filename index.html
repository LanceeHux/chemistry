01 c. 460–370 BCE

Democritus

The Atomos — “that which cannot be cut”

Democritus proposed that all matter is made of tiny, indivisible particles called atomos, meaning or “indivisible.” Democritus was an ancient Greek philosopher, a central figure in the development of philosophical atomism and of the atomic theory of the universe. He is also the first person who use the term “atom”

02 1803

John Dalton

The Solid Sphere Model

Dalton drew upon the Ancient Greek idea of atoms (the word 'atom' comes from the Greek 'atomos' meaning indivisible). His theory stated that atoms are indivisible, those of a given element are identical, and compounds are combinations of different types of atoms. - Also John Dalton recognized that atoms of a particular element differ from other elements

03 1897

J.J. Thomson

The Plum Pudding Model

J.J. Thomson proposed the Plum Pudding Model in 1904 after his discovery of the electron. -According to this model, the atom was thought to be a sphere of positively charged matter with negatively charged electrons embedded throughout it. Thomson believed that the positive charge was spread evenly across the atom, while the electrons were distributed within it. - The model was called the “Plum Pudding Model” because it was compared to a dessert containing raisins or plums. In this analogy, the positive charge represented the pudding, while the negatively charged electrons represented the plums or raisins scattered throughout it.

04 1911

Ernest Rutherford

The Nuclear Model

He discovered the proton. He believed that the atom had a massive, positively charged particle at its center. He also believed that electrons were outside the center of the atom and moved around it like planets around the sun.

05 1913

Niels Bohr

The Planetary Model

Bohr took Rutherford's nucleus and added a rule: electrons don't drift freely, they occupy fixed, quantized orbits, jumping between them only by absorbing or releasing exact packets of energy. This finally explained the sharp, discrete lines seen in hydrogen's emission spectrum — and became the bridge from classical physics into the quantum age.

Erwin Schrödinger

The Quantum Mechanical Model

He's the one who used Heisenberg's uncertainty principle to come up with the atomic model that we still use today. - He stated that electrons do not move in set paths around the nucleus, but in waves. - It is impossible to know the exact location of the electrons. - Instead, we had clouds of probability called orbitals, in which we are more likely to find an electron (The Electron Cloud Model).
